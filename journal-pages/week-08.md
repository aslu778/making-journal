## Week 08 – Process Documentation
Overview
This week focused on presenting my progress, receiving structured feedback, and engaging in critical design exchange. These activities helped me refine my project direction and push the conceptual and visual clarity of my dual-layer commuting visualisation.

1. Progress Reports
Presentation Summary
I presented my project as a dual-layer visualisation comparing:

Hand-drawn commuting data (subjective, lived experience)
A speculative smart mobility dashboard (structured, optimised system view)

I emphasised the idea that methodology itself acts as critique, showing how systems flatten human experience.

Feedback Received
Conceptual Feedback

The idea of data sovereignty is strong, but could be pushed further
The critique works best when the system feels believable, not too exaggerated
The “disappearing drawing” idea was seen as particularly powerful

Technical/Design Feedback

The dashboard needs a more distinct visual identity (fonts, hierarchy, colour)
Consider how users will understand the drawing without explanation
Interaction should feel intuitive and meaningful, not just a visual toggle


Key Takeaways

Balance between realism and exaggeration is crucial
Interface design = part of the argument, not just presentation
Time/transition (not just comparison) makes the concept stronger

## Independent Study
1. Reflective Summary (≈320 words)
The most significant feedback I received during the progress report and peer exchange focused on the importance of refining the visual language and conceptual clarity of my dashboard. While my dual-layer approach was understood and well received, it became clear that the success of the project depends heavily on how convincingly the speculative system is designed. If the dashboard appears too neutral, the critique is weakened; if it is too exaggerated, it risks losing credibility. Moving forward, I will focus on developing a carefully balanced aesthetic that signals institutional authority while subtly exposing its reductive logic.
The suggestion to explore time-based transformation, particularly the idea of the hand-drawn layer gradually disappearing, stood out as a strong direction. This shifts the project from a static comparison to a narrative experience, making the loss of nuance more visible and impactful. I have decided to integrate this into my design, potentially through animation or staged interaction.
Another key insight was the need to support the readability of the hand-drawn data. While ambiguity is intentional, some level of guidance will help users engage more meaningfully with the visualisation.
In response to these ideas, my next steps will focus on refining the dashboard interface, experimenting with transitions between layers, and testing how users interpret the data. These decisions will help strengthen both the conceptual argument and the user experience of the project.

2. Project Development & Skill Building
What I Worked On

Refined dashboard layout (grid, metrics, labels)
Tested layering of hand-drawn + digital visuals
Explored simple animation ideas (fade/overlay)


What I Tried

Designing a more “official-looking” UI (boxes, metrics, system labels)
Overlaying drawings with transparent digital elements
Testing fading effects between layers


What I Learned

Authority is communicated through:

Alignment
Consistent spacing
Neutral colour palettes


Small UI details (labels, numbers) make the system feel real
Transitions are key to storytelling


How This Moves My Project Forward

I now have a clearer direction for the dashboard aesthetic
I am moving from static visuals → interactive/narrative experience
My concept is becoming more visually legible and conceptually precise
