## Week 11 – Process Documentation
Overview
This week focused on reviewing and refining my process documentation, preparing for the Studio Consultation, and finalising plans for the Week 12 showcase. Through peer review and practice consultations, I reflected on how effectively I communicate my project concept and its positioning within broader ideas about data.

1. Journal Review
Peer Feedback on My Journal (Weeks 6–10)
Strengths:

Clear development from idea → prototype → refined concept
Strong balance of text and visuals (sketches, UI, variations)
Good reflection on feedback and decision-making

Areas to Improve:

Some entries could have more concise summaries
Could highlight key turning points more clearly
Add clearer captions for visuals


Key Moments That Shaped My Project
1. Discovering the Dual-Layer Concept
Developing the idea of placing hand-drawn data alongside a speculative dashboard shifted my project from simple visualisation to a critical design argument about data interpretation.
2. Feedback on Dashboard Aesthetic
The consultation highlighted the importance of visual authority, leading me to refine the dashboard’s grid, hierarchy, and metrics to make it feel institutional and believable.
3. Introducing the “Disappearing Drawing”
Exploring the gradual removal of hand-drawn data transformed the project into a narrative experience, strengthening its emotional and critical impact.

2. Practice Consultations
Reflection on My Responses
During the mock consultations, I was able to explain:

My concept clearly (datafication + commuting)
My methodology (self-collected, interpretive data)
My visual system (dual-layer interface)

However, I realised that:

I sometimes spoke too generally about “data systems”
I could be more specific when explaining data sovereignty
My explanation of interaction could be clearer and more concise


What I Want to Improve

Use more precise language when describing my argument
Clearly explain how the interface works in simple terms
Connect my project more directly to broader issues of power and control in data


3. Showcase Planning

Selected a position on the Miro board for installation
Planned to display:

Digital interface (main work)
Supporting visuals (drawings + process)



Installation plan:

Laptop or screen for interactive piece
Printed hand-drawn visuals alongside
Clear title + short statement near the work


Independent Study – Finalisation
Final Development Work
What I Refined

Final dashboard UI (spacing, typography, clarity)
Hand-drawn visuals (cleaner but still expressive)
Transition effect (smooth disappearance of data)


What I Tried

Adjusting opacity and timing for fade effect
Simplifying interface interactions
Adding subtle guidance labels for users


What I Learned

Strong work depends on clarity and refinement, not adding more elements
Interaction must be immediately understandable
Small visual decisions have a large impact on meaning


Final Direction
My project is now a clear, cohesive interactive visualisation that:

Contrasts human and system interpretations of data
Uses transition (disappearance) as its core narrative device
Critiques optimisation and data authority
